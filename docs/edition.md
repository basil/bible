# Edition and upstream findings

Research was conducted against [PTXprint release 3.0.43](https://github.com/sillsdev/ptx2pdf/tree/3.0.43), commit `f4409d06e6664cab14bd7afbde3d49eac47c0935`, and the source snapshots retrieved on 2026-09-23. The mutable upstream pages are informational; the checked-in archives are the build inputs.

- [Brenton](https://ebible.org/eng-Brenton/) / [USFM](https://ebible.org/Scriptures/eng-Brenton_usfm.zip): eBible's transcription, including corrections to the 1870 text. Its `XXC` Errata describes those corrections and the normalization of added-word punctuation. This is neither a facsimile nor the separate Updated Brenton translation.
- [Cambridge Paragraph KJV](https://ebible.org/engkjvcpb/) / [USFM](https://ebible.org/Scriptures/engkjvcpb_usfm.zip): `engkjvcpb`, not `eng-kjv2006`. The 27 NT books, translators' preface (`INT`), and dedication (`OTH`) are selected. The historical 1873 title page (`FRT`) is omitted by editorial choice. This snapshot has no footnotes or cross-reference markers in its NT; the pipeline does not invent them. It retains its italics, paragraphing, poetry, and numbering.
- Both distributions identify their text as public domain. The KJV source notice additionally records the UK Crown printing rights. Exact notices are retained in the archives (`copr.htm`) and as downloaded HTML.

`config/edition.json` is the ordered scripture manifest. The Old Testament follows the [Church of Greece's book list](https://apostoliki-diakonia.gr/bible/zacharias/) in English, with Brenton's book names retained except for the specified Orthodox names and Arabic-number title styling. Prayer of Manasses follows 2 Chronicles, and 4 Maccabees forms an Old Testament appendix before Matthew. There is no separate Apocrypha reading block. Psalm 151, Esther's additions, and Daniel 3's additions remain in their source passages.

The combined `EZR` file has 23 chapters. Its first ten appear as 2 Esdras; chapters 11–23 appear as Nehemiah 1–13. The overlapping standalone `NEH` file has 13 chapters and remains excluded. They are not byte-identical duplicate witnesses: differences include paragraph divisions, initial small capitals, ligature spellings (for example Raphæa/Raphaea), and verse labels (`EZR` 13:6a versus `NEH` 3:7). `make validate` writes a full comparison to `build/nehemiah-differences.diff`, renumbering the combined chapters only within that diagnostic comparison. Neither source is altered.

Susanna and Bel and the Dragon are titled sections within Daniel, placed before Daniel 1 and after Daniel 12. Following the Church of Greece's online chapter sequence, their internal project labels are Daniel 0 and 13; the numbers are suppressed beside the passages and their names appear in running headers. The addition within Daniel 3 is headed “Song of Azarias and Hymn of the Three Youths.” Its Brenton verse labels remain intact: unlike the Church of Greece presentation, Brenton integrates the addition into Daniel 3:24–90, merges some of their verse units, and lacks the duplicated units they number 44–45. Relabeling it as their separate verses 1–67 would therefore misstate textual equivalence. Brenton's Malachias 3:19-24 is relabeled 4:1-6, including its note reference, matching the [Church of Greece's fourth chapter](https://apostoliki-diakonia.gr/bible/malachias/?file=42.4). These changes affect generated files only.

Brenton's abbreviations and 1870 introduction precede scripture. Immediately after that introduction comes the complete KJV [Epistle Dedicatory](https://ebible.org/engkjvcpb/OTH01.htm), beginning "Great and manifold were the blessings". This is the dedication to King James, distinct from "The Translators to the Reader". It is already present in the pinned Cambridge archive. Its project ID becomes `TDX` to avoid colliding with Brenton's `OTH` introduction to the Apocrypha. The dedication is followed by the complete "The Translators to the Reader" (`INT` - `NDX`). The traditional epistle subscriptions and "The end of the Acts of the Apostles" remain in their original books. Scrivener's critical introduction and scholarly appendices are not in this digital package, despite the historical title page advertising them; the new source statement makes that limitation explicit. Brenton's introduction to the Apocrypha begins the historical apparatus after Revelation, followed by the Jeremiah correspondence table, 1844 preface, Errata, and Appendix. The Appendix's source chapter and printed verse markers are retained, even though this unit is historical apparatus, not a scripture book. Historical claims remain attributed to their original authors.

## PTXprint integration

PTXprint's CLI imports GTK-related modules even for headless printing. The image therefore includes GTK 3, GObject introspection, Cairo, and Poppler runtime bindings. `print`, `-N`, and `-q` select offline headless typesetting. The runtime network namespace is disabled independently of `-N`.

The release's Python metadata points at `usfmtc`'s moving `main.zip`. Installation deliberately uses `--no-deps --no-build-isolation`, after installing a separately pinned `usfmtc` checkout and the versioned Python dependencies in `requirements.txt`. The PTXprint tag is shallow-cloned during image construction and its resolved commit checked before installation. Both checkouts remain in the image, never in this repository.

The original BSB project is used only for its layout configuration. Its book files and inherited publication metadata are not copied into the combined project. `config/layout.ini` is an overlay that replaces Charis with Utopia; `config/ptxprint-mods.sty` supplies GFS Porson for `wg` and Ezra SIL for `wh`. Preparation explicitly tags Greek/Hebrew quotation runs, including single Hebrew letters missed by PTXprint's automatic heuristic; its RTL `wh` hooks are made explicit in `config/ptxprint-mods.tex`. Utopia is built from the pinned Adobe Utopia Type 1 repository during image construction.

Preparation removes an optional UTF-8 BOM and normalizes CRLF line endings. To avoid PTXprint treating Brenton's original `FRT` and `INT` as replacement front matter, their project IDs become `XXD` and `XXE`; their wording and remaining markers are unchanged. Literal vertical bars in the Errata are temporarily protected with U+E000 during PTXprint's XX-module parsing and restored to U+007C afterwards; otherwise the USFM parser silently discards text as attributes. The final processed text is compared word-for-word and punctuation-for-punctuation, ignoring only whitespace and markup. These changes are recorded in `build/<run>/transformations.json`. New peripheral IDs hold the section dividers. Source archives remain untouched. PTXprint's canonicalization option is disabled to avoid silently revising source markup.

One reference-only footnote in `1KI` 6:1 has no note body. PTXprint otherwise removes it as empty. Preparation appends a nonprinting U+200B spacer under `ft` so that the original caller and `fr` reference survive; the source archive remains unchanged. This case is included in the sample. The upstream study-Bible stylesheet also defaults to bold italic additions; this edition overrides `add` to ordinary italics. The apparently inverted `fnomitcaller` and `xromitcaller` configuration values are intentionally `True`: in this release that comments out `OmitCallerInNote`, retaining matching callers in both text and notes.

The build environment uses the latest image under Ubuntu's floating `26.04` tag. Moving from Debian Bookworm to Ubuntu 26.04 also moves the system Python from 3.11 to 3.14. Regex, Pillow, NumPy, and setuptools were updated to releases with Python 3.14 support; their versions are recorded in `requirements.txt`.
