# Edition and upstream findings

Research was conducted against [PTXprint release 3.0.43](https://github.com/sillsdev/ptx2pdf/tree/3.0.43), commit `f4409d06e6664cab14bd7afbde3d49eac47c0935`, and the source snapshots retrieved on 2026-09-23. The mutable upstream pages are informational; the checked-in archives are the build inputs.

- [Brenton](https://ebible.org/eng-Brenton/) / [USFM](https://ebible.org/Scriptures/eng-Brenton_usfm.zip): eBible's transcription, including corrections to the 1870 text. Its `XXC` Errata (printed here as Corrections to the Text, the last appendix) describes those corrections and the normalization of added-word punctuation. This is neither a facsimile nor the separate Updated Brenton translation.
- [Cambridge Paragraph KJV](https://ebible.org/engkjvcpb/) / [USFM](https://ebible.org/Scriptures/engkjvcpb_usfm.zip): `engkjvcpb`, not `eng-kjv2006`. The 27 NT books, translators' preface (`INT`), and dedication (`OTH`) are selected. The historical 1873 title page (`FRT`) is omitted by editorial choice. This snapshot has no footnotes or cross-reference markers in its NT; the footnotes printed there are the 1611 marginal notes described below. It retains its italics, paragraphing, poetry, and numbering.
- [1611 KJV marginal notes](https://en.literaturabautista.com/exhaustive-listing-marginal-notes-1611-edition-king-james-bible): Calvin George's modern-spelling transcription, saved as Markdown in `sources/`. Only its 775 New Testament notes are read. Its Old Testament notes belong to the Hebrew Old Testament of the KJV, which this edition does not print.
- Both distributions identify their text as public domain. The KJV source notice additionally records the UK Crown printing rights. Exact notices are retained in the archives (`copr.htm`) and as downloaded HTML.

`config/edition.json` lists the books in order. The Old Testament follows the [Church of Greece's book list](https://apostoliki-diakonia.gr/bible/zacharias/) in English. Brenton's book names are kept except where the edition substitutes an Orthodox one: Jesus, the Son of Navi; 1-4 Kingdoms; the Song of Songs; the Lamentations of Jeremias; Ezekiel; and Michaias. Numbered books put the number first, as in 1 Chronicles. The Prayer of Manasses follows 2 Chronicles, and 4 Maccabees follows 3 Maccabees. There is no separate Apocrypha section or Old Testament appendix; Psalm 151, the additions to Esther, and the additions to Daniel 3 stay where their sources put them.

Each book in the manifest carries up to three names and an optional heading layout, which the pipeline writes into the standard USFM markers and into `BookNames.xml`:

- `title` is the full title, written to `toc1` and printed in the table of contents. It follows the wording of the Cambridge KJV, adapted to the edition's names, and carries the same pattern on to the books the Cambridge Paragraph Bible lacks or names differently, such as the Esdras books and 3-4 Maccabees: "The First Book of Moses, Called Genesis", "The Book of Jesus, the Son of Navi", "The Third Book of Kingdoms, Otherwise Called, The First Book of the Kings", "The Wisdom of Jesus, the Son of Sirach, or, Ecclesiasticus". Every book has one, and `make validate` checks each Brenton title against the list of revised titles in `scripts/pipeline.py` or, for a book left as Brenton named it, against his own `toc1`.
- `short_title` is the short name, written to `toc2` and to `h`, so it is what the running headers show. It uses Arabic numerals, as in "3 Kingdoms". When it is omitted, the source's `toc2` is used, which is why Genesis needs no entry.
- `abbreviation` is written to `toc3`. It is not printed, since `config/layout.ini` sets `usetoc3 = False`, but it is kept consistent. When omitted, the source's `toc3` is used; where one is set, it mostly follows the Cambridge KJV's form respelled for the edition's name, such as "Deut.", "Eccles.", "Esai.", and "Prayer of Manass.".
- `heading` lays the printed heading out over `mt1`-`mt3` lines. It is a list of marker and text pairs whose texts, joined with spaces, must spell the `title` exactly, with exactly one `mt1` for the main line. It follows the Cambridge KJV's layout where there is one to follow, adapted to this edition's names: Genesis is "The First Book of Moses," over "Called" over "Genesis", and 1 Kingdoms is "The" over "First Book of Kingdoms," over "Otherwise Called," over "The First Book of Samuel". A book without it is headed by its whole title on one `mt1` line.

Every Brenton book is therefore headed by its contents title, as the Cambridge books are by theirs, and Brenton's own subtitle lines such as "(1 SAMUEL)" and "(ECCLESIASTICUS)" give way to the layout. Where that rule and the Cambridge punctuation part company, the title wins: Sirach keeps a comma after "Jesus" to match "Jesus, the Son of Navi", and the Manasses heading takes the comma of its title. The eBible transcription of the Cambridge Sirach heading misspells "SIRACH"; this edition sets its own lines and does not inherit it. In the New Testament, "S." is expanded to "Saint", Paul the Apostle becomes Saint Paul, and the Catholic Epistles are titled after the Greek tradition. Closing periods are removed from all titles and headings. Every change to a heading is recorded in `build/<run>/transformations.json`.

The combined `EZR` file is titled "Ezra and Nehemiah" in the source and has 23 chapters. Its first ten appear as 2 Esdras; chapters 11-23 appear as Nehemias 1-13. The overlapping standalone `NEH` file is titled "Nehemiah" in the source, has 13 chapters, and remains excluded. They are not byte-identical duplicate witnesses: differences include paragraph divisions, initial small capitals, ligature spellings (for example Raphæa/Raphaea), and verse labels (`EZR` 13:6a versus `NEH` 3:7). `make validate` writes a full comparison to `build/nehemias-differences.diff`, renumbering the combined chapters only within that diagnostic comparison. Neither source is altered.

Susanna and Bel and the Dragon are titled sections within Daniel, placed before Daniel 1 and after Daniel 12. Following the Church of Greece's online chapter sequence, their internal project labels are Daniel 0 and 13; the numbers are suppressed beside the passages with a zero-width `\cp`, for which `config/ptxprint-mods.tex` reserves no drop-cap space, while running headers use PTXprint's standard reference formatting. The addition within Daniel 3 is headed "The Song of the Three Children," following the name used in Brenton's introduction. Its Brenton verse labels remain intact: unlike the Church of Greece presentation, Brenton integrates the addition into Daniel 3:24-90, merges some of their verse units, and lacks the duplicated units they number 44-45. Relabeling it as their separate verses 1-67 would therefore misstate textual equivalence. Brenton's Malachias 3:19-24 is relabeled 4:1-6, including its note reference, matching the [Church of Greece's fourth chapter](https://apostoliki-diakonia.gr/bible/malachias/?file=42.4). These changes affect generated files only.

The contents page follows the title page, as in most Bibles and in Chicago-style front matter, and the editor's introduction comes after it. The introduction is therefore a project unit (`CNC`, from `config/introduction.sfm`) rather than a periph in `config/front.sfm`, so it is listed in the contents and paginated like any other unit. Each testament opens with its divider and the front matter of its own translation, so that Brenton's introductions and abbreviations sit next to the text and notes they serve, and the King James material next to the New Testament. The Old Testament divider is followed by Brenton's preface, introduction, introduction to the Apocrypha, and list of abbreviations, in that order, then Genesis. The preface (1844) briefly explains and defends translating the Septuagint into English; the introduction (1870) gives a longer history of the version; the introduction to the Apocrypha describes books that are interleaved in the Old Testament, so it sits with the other introductions rather than after Revelation. Its source heading, "The Books of the Apocrypha", is retained. The New Testament divider is followed by the complete KJV [Epistle Dedicatory](https://ebible.org/engkjvcpb/OTH01.htm), beginning "Great and manifold were the blessings", then the complete "The Translators to the Reader" (`INT` - `NDX`), then Matthew. The dedication is the address to King James, distinct from the translators' preface; it is already present in the pinned Cambridge archive, and its project ID becomes `TDX` to avoid colliding with Brenton's `OTH` Introduction to the Apocrypha. The traditional epistle subscriptions and "The end of the Acts of the Apostles" remain in their original books. Scrivener's critical introduction and scholarly appendices are not in this digital package, despite the historical title page advertising them; the editor's introduction makes that limitation explicit. The appendices after Revelation are the Jeremias correspondence table, Brenton's Appendix (printed as Notes and Supplied Passages), and eBible's Errata (printed as Corrections to the Text), in that order, so that Brenton's own material precedes eBible's. The notes unit keeps its source chapter and printed verse markers even though it is apparatus, not a scripture book. Printed headings that differ from the source (the preface and introduction with their years in parentheses, the shortened Jeremias table title, Notes and Supplied Passages, and Corrections to the Text) are set in `config/edition.json` under `headings`; the divider titles and subtitles are in `scripts/pipeline.py`. Historical claims remain attributed to their original authors.

The 1611 marginal notes are set as footnotes in the Cambridge New Testament, in Brenton's footnote style: `\f + \fr 6:2 \fqa Or, \ft cause not a trumpet to be sounded.\f*`. A leading "Or," or "Gr." is set as `fqa`, and a closing full stop is added where the note has none. The glossed words are not repeated in the note. As in Brenton and in the 1611 printing, the caller stands before the words the note concerns. George gives those words for every note, and preparation places the caller before the one place in the verse where they occur, comparing words without regard to case, punctuation, apostrophes, or markup. It never places a caller inside an added-word span. For 61 notes that comparison does not find a single place, and `config/marginal-notes.json` gives the Cambridge words instead:
- The spelling differs from George's, as in boysterous, council, and thyine.
- The words occur more than once in the verse, such as "of" in Matthew 6:1, and the anchor gives the occurrence. Each choice follows the sense of the note and has its reason recorded.
- George gives the wrong verse. Two notes that he lists under 1 Corinthians 10:6 and Galatians 4:24 gloss words in 10:4 and 4:25.

The same file records seven corrections to note bodies:
- George's bracketed remarks are removed (Matthew 5:15, Mark 14:72, Revelation 20:13). Mark 14:72 keeps the 1611's own "began to wept", and Revelation 20:13 its "Or, hell".
- The Greek he left out is restored from the 1611 margin (Acts 13:18, 13:34).
- Two slips are fixed: "debtOr" (Matthew 23:18) and "Ceasars" (Philippians 1:13, spelled Cesar as in 1611 and in this text).

Every placement and correction is recorded in `build/<run>/transformations.json`. `make validate` requires the pinned hash and all 775 notes. It also requires that each note resolves to exactly one place, that every anchor and correction in the configuration is used, that no bracketed remark remains, and that removing the added notes gives back the unchanged Cambridge text.

## PTXprint integration

PTXprint's CLI imports GTK-related modules even for headless printing. The image therefore includes GTK 3, GObject introspection, Cairo, and Poppler runtime bindings. `print`, `-N`, and `-q` select offline headless typesetting. The runtime network namespace is disabled independently of `-N`.

The release's Python metadata points at `usfmtc`'s moving `main.zip`. Installation deliberately uses `--no-deps --no-build-isolation`, after installing a separately pinned `usfmtc` checkout and the versioned Python dependencies in `requirements.txt`. The PTXprint tag is shallow-cloned during image construction and its resolved commit checked before installation. Both checkouts remain in the image, never in this repository.

The original BSB project is used for its layout configuration and stylesheet. Its book files and inherited publication metadata are not copied into the combined project. `config/layout.ini` is an overlay that replaces Charis with Utopia; `config/ptxprint-mods.sty` supplies GFS Porson for `wg` and sets added words in regular type. Hebrew `wh` styling inherits Ezra SIL from PTXprint. Preparation explicitly tags Greek/Hebrew quotation runs, including single Hebrew letters missed by PTXprint's automatic heuristic; right-to-left direction uses PTXprint's built-in `wh` hooks. Utopia is built from the pinned Adobe Utopia Type 1 repository during image construction.

Preparation removes an optional UTF-8 BOM and normalizes CRLF line endings. To avoid PTXprint treating Brenton's original `FRT` and `INT` as replacement front matter, their project IDs become `XXD` and `XXE`; their wording and remaining markers are unchanged. Literal vertical bars in the Errata are temporarily protected with U+E000 during PTXprint's XX-module parsing and restored to U+007C afterwards; otherwise the USFM parser silently discards text as attributes. The final processed text is compared word-for-word and punctuation-for-punctuation, ignoring only whitespace and markup. These changes are recorded in `build/<run>/transformations.json`. New peripheral IDs hold the section dividers. Source archives remain untouched. PTXprint's canonicalization option is disabled to avoid silently revising source markup.

One reference-only footnote in `1KI` 6:1 has no note body. PTXprint otherwise removes it as empty. Preparation appends a nonprinting U+200B spacer under `ft` so that the original caller and `fr` reference survive; the source archive remains unchanged. This case is included in the sample. The upstream study-Bible stylesheet defaults to bold italic additions; this edition overrides `add` to regular type. The apparently inverted `fnomitcaller` and `xromitcaller` configuration values are intentionally `True`: in this release that comments out `OmitCallerInNote`, retaining matching callers in both text and notes. BSB has no `\x` notes and sets no caller sequences, so footnotes and cross-references would share PTXprint's a-z fallback; `config/layout.ini` gives cross-references their own symbols.

The build environment uses the latest image under Ubuntu's floating `26.04` tag. Moving from Debian Bookworm to Ubuntu 26.04 also moves the system Python from 3.11 to 3.14. Regex, Pillow, NumPy, and setuptools were updated to releases with Python 3.14 support; their versions are recorded in `requirements.txt`.
