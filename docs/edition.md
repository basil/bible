# Edition and upstream findings

Research was conducted against [PTXprint release 3.0.43](https://github.com/sillsdev/ptx2pdf/tree/3.0.43), commit `f4409d06e6664cab14bd7afbde3d49eac47c0935`, and the source snapshots retrieved on 2026-09-23. The mutable upstream pages are informational; the checked-in archives are the build inputs.

- [Brenton](https://ebible.org/eng-Brenton/) / [USFM](https://ebible.org/Scriptures/eng-Brenton_usfm.zip): eBible's transcription, including corrections to the 1870 text. Its `XXC` Errata (printed here as Corrections to the Text, the last appendix) describes those corrections and the normalization of added-word punctuation. This is neither a facsimile nor the separate Updated Brenton translation.
- [Cambridge Paragraph KJV](https://ebible.org/engkjvcpb/) / [USFM](https://ebible.org/Scriptures/engkjvcpb_usfm.zip): `engkjvcpb`, not `eng-kjv2006`. The 27 NT books, translators' preface (`INT`), and dedication (`OTH`) are selected. The historical 1873 title page (`FRT`) is omitted by editorial choice. This snapshot has no footnotes or cross-reference markers in its NT; the pipeline does not invent them. It retains its italics, paragraphing, poetry, and numbering.
- Both distributions identify their text as public domain. The KJV source notice additionally records the UK Crown printing rights. Exact notices are retained in the archives (`copr.htm`) and as downloaded HTML.

`config/edition.json` is the ordered scripture manifest. The Old Testament follows the [Church of Greece's book list](https://apostoliki-diakonia.gr/bible/zacharias/) in English, with Brenton's book names retained except for the specified Orthodox names and Arabic-number title styling. Prayer of Manasses follows 2 Chronicles, and 4 Maccabees follows 3 Maccabees. There is no separate Apocrypha reading block or Old Testament appendix. Psalm 151, Esther's additions, and Daniel 3's additions remain in their source passages.

The combined `EZR` file has 23 chapters. Its first ten appear as 2 Esdras; chapters 11–23 appear as Nehemiah 1–13. The overlapping standalone `NEH` file has 13 chapters and remains excluded. They are not byte-identical duplicate witnesses: differences include paragraph divisions, initial small capitals, ligature spellings (for example Raphæa/Raphaea), and verse labels (`EZR` 13:6a versus `NEH` 3:7). `make validate` writes a full comparison to `build/nehemiah-differences.diff`, renumbering the combined chapters only within that diagnostic comparison. Neither source is altered.

Susanna and Bel and the Dragon are titled sections within Daniel, placed before Daniel 1 and after Daniel 12. Following the Church of Greece's online chapter sequence, their internal project labels are Daniel 0 and 13; the numbers are suppressed beside the passages, while running headers use PTXprint’s standard reference formatting. The addition within Daniel 3 is headed “The Song of the Three Children,” following the name used in Brenton's introduction. Its Brenton verse labels remain intact: unlike the Church of Greece presentation, Brenton integrates the addition into Daniel 3:24–90, merges some of their verse units, and lacks the duplicated units they number 44–45. Relabeling it as their separate verses 1–67 would therefore misstate textual equivalence. Brenton's Malachias 3:19-24 is relabeled 4:1-6, including its note reference, matching the [Church of Greece's fourth chapter](https://apostoliki-diakonia.gr/bible/malachias/?file=42.4). These changes affect generated files only.

The contents page follows the title page, as in most Bibles and in Chicago-style front matter, and the editor's introduction comes after it. The introduction is therefore a project unit (`CNC`, from `config/introduction.sfm`) rather than a periph in `config/front.sfm`, so it is listed in the contents and paginated like any other unit. Each testament opens with its divider and the front matter of its own translation, so that Brenton's introductions and abbreviations sit next to the text and notes they serve, and the King James material next to the New Testament. The Old Testament divider is followed by Brenton's preface, introduction, introduction to the Apocrypha, and list of abbreviations, in that order, then Genesis. The preface (1844) briefly explains and defends translating the Septuagint into English; the introduction (1870) gives a longer history of the version; the introduction to the Apocrypha describes books that are interleaved in the Old Testament, so it sits with the other introductions rather than after Revelation. Its source heading, "The Books of the Apocrypha", is retained. The New Testament divider is followed by the complete KJV [Epistle Dedicatory](https://ebible.org/engkjvcpb/OTH01.htm), beginning "Great and manifold were the blessings", then the complete "The Translators to the Reader" (`INT` - `NDX`), then Matthew. The dedication is the address to King James, distinct from the translators' preface; it is already present in the pinned Cambridge archive, and its project ID becomes `TDX` to avoid colliding with Brenton's `OTH` Introduction to the Apocrypha. The traditional epistle subscriptions and "The end of the Acts of the Apostles" remain in their original books. Scrivener's critical introduction and scholarly appendices are not in this digital package, despite the historical title page advertising them; the editor's introduction makes that limitation explicit. The appendices after Revelation are the Jeremiah correspondence table, Brenton's Appendix (printed as Notes and Supplied Passages), and eBible's Errata (printed as Corrections to the Text), in that order, so that Brenton's own material precedes eBible's. The notes unit keeps its source chapter and printed verse markers even though it is apparatus, not a scripture book. Printed headings that differ from the source (the preface and introduction with their years in parentheses, the shortened Jeremiah table title, Notes and Supplied Passages, and Corrections to the Text) are set in `config/edition.json` under `headings`; the divider titles and subtitles are in `scripts/pipeline.py`. Historical claims remain attributed to their original authors.

## PTXprint integration

PTXprint's CLI imports GTK-related modules even for headless printing. The image therefore includes GTK 3, GObject introspection, Cairo, and Poppler runtime bindings. `print`, `-N`, and `-q` select offline headless typesetting. The runtime network namespace is disabled independently of `-N`.

The release's Python metadata points at `usfmtc`'s moving `main.zip`. Installation deliberately uses `--no-deps --no-build-isolation`, after installing a separately pinned `usfmtc` checkout and the versioned Python dependencies in `requirements.txt`. The PTXprint tag is shallow-cloned during image construction and its resolved commit checked before installation. Both checkouts remain in the image, never in this repository.

The original BSB project is used for its layout configuration and stylesheet. Its book files and inherited publication metadata are not copied into the combined project. `config/layout.ini` is an overlay that replaces Charis with Utopia; `config/ptxprint-mods.sty` supplies GFS Porson for `wg` and sets added words in regular type. Hebrew `wh` styling inherits Ezra SIL from PTXprint. Preparation explicitly tags Greek/Hebrew quotation runs, including single Hebrew letters missed by PTXprint's automatic heuristic; right-to-left direction uses PTXprint’s built-in `wh` hooks. Utopia is built from the pinned Adobe Utopia Type 1 repository during image construction.

Preparation removes an optional UTF-8 BOM and normalizes CRLF line endings. To avoid PTXprint treating Brenton's original `FRT` and `INT` as replacement front matter, their project IDs become `XXD` and `XXE`; their wording and remaining markers are unchanged. Literal vertical bars in the Errata are temporarily protected with U+E000 during PTXprint's XX-module parsing and restored to U+007C afterwards; otherwise the USFM parser silently discards text as attributes. The final processed text is compared word-for-word and punctuation-for-punctuation, ignoring only whitespace and markup. These changes are recorded in `build/<run>/transformations.json`. New peripheral IDs hold the section dividers. Source archives remain untouched. PTXprint's canonicalization option is disabled to avoid silently revising source markup.

One reference-only footnote in `1KI` 6:1 has no note body. PTXprint otherwise removes it as empty. Preparation appends a nonprinting U+200B spacer under `ft` so that the original caller and `fr` reference survive; the source archive remains unchanged. This case is included in the sample. The upstream study-Bible stylesheet defaults to bold italic additions; this edition overrides `add` to regular type. The apparently inverted `fnomitcaller` and `xromitcaller` configuration values are intentionally `True`: in this release that comments out `OmitCallerInNote`, retaining matching callers in both text and notes.

The build environment uses the latest image under Ubuntu's floating `26.04` tag. Moving from Debian Bookworm to Ubuntu 26.04 also moves the system Python from 3.11 to 3.14. Regex, Pillow, NumPy, and setuptools were updated to releases with Python 3.14 support; their versions are recorded in `requirements.txt`.
