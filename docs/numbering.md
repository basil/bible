# The Old Testament's numbering

This page says how the Old Testament's chapters and verses are numbered, and why. [Editorial notes](edition.md#numbering) say what the numbers are for: every reference in the edition uses them. Places where a witness numbers otherwise but the rule keeps the edition's numbers, and that need a word of explanation, are listed under [Places the rule leaves as they are](#places-the-rule-leaves-as-they-are).

## The goal

The edition is an Orthodox Septuagint, so it uses the Septuagint's numbering. A reader should find a verse under the number a Greek Orthodox Bible or a Septuagint edition gives it, and never under the Masoretic Hebrew's number unless the Septuagint's editions use it too. The Apocrypha are printed among the other books, as the Greek sets them, and are numbered by the same rule, not by the King James Apocrypha's division.

The numbers must also fit the edition's text, which is Brenton's English with some readings from Codex Alexandrinus. A number belongs to words, and dividing a verse again moves Brenton's notes, the quotation links, and the lines of verse with it. So a verse is divided again only on strong evidence.

## Where the numbers come from

The text, and so its numbers, comes from eBible's transcription of Brenton. eBible does not use Brenton's printed numbers. It numbers his English as Rahlfs numbers the Greek, in 94% of chapters, and has letters and slips of its own: it letters verses Brenton numbers (Tobit 4:7a–l for his 4:8–18), and drops or splits a few verse numbers (Jeremias 34:17). Brenton's printed pages often differ from Rahlfs, mostly at about 25 chapter breaks where Rahlfs follows the Hebrew and Brenton, Swete, and the King James Bible do not (Genesis 31/32, Exodus 21/22, Jeremias 8/9, and others). So the question is not how to restore Brenton's numbers from a few slips. It is which Septuagint numbering to print, and on what evidence.

## The witnesses

- **Brenton's printed pages:** the Bagster *Septuagint Version* of 1879, with the 1900 printing for the Apocrypha, and the 1844 edition where Bagster is unclear. Only the scans are evidence of what he printed. eBible substituted Rahlfs's divisions and CCEL normalized some numbers, so both transcriptions are used only to find the page. The English column's numbers count, because they divide the words the edition prints; the Greek column's do not. Some of Brenton's numbers are borrowed, and a borrowed number says nothing about the Septuagint:
  - his Apocrypha is the King James Apocrypha, revised in places, with its verse division (3 and 4 Maccabees, which the King James Apocrypha lacks, are numbered by Brenton himself);
  - in 3 Kingdoms 4–11 he prints the King James numbers out of order;
  - in Jeremias 25–51 he prints the Hebrew's numbers;
  - in the Psalms he does not count a psalm's title, and he restarts Psalms 9 and 113 at the Hebrew's break;
  - in Proverbs 24–31 he prints the King James numbers in the Greek order.
- **Swete**, *The Old Testament in Greek according to the Septuagint* (3rd edition, 1901–1907), in the corrected transcription of the sibling `swete` repository, checked against the scans where the transcription is ambiguous. Swete prints Codex B and numbers it in the older tradition Brenton and the King James Bible share. Where B lacks a verse, Swete leaves its number vacant, and that vacant number counts as his number for the verse. His numbers in parentheses, mostly the Hebrew's, do not count.
- **The Church of Greece's text**, as the Apostoliki Diakonia publishes it (apostoliki-diakonia.gr): the Greek Orthodox Church's text. Its numbering is Rahlfs's in 86% of chapters, and it departs from Rahlfs mostly toward the traditional breaks Brenton and Swete share. Its website has many slips, such as "9" for 19, dropped numerals, and a chapter's first verse printed as the last of the chapter before. So a reading only it has is not trusted.
- **Rahlfs's *Septuaginta*** (1935), the modern standard edition.

Every place where the edition differed from Swete was read on Brenton's pages. The Church of Greece's whole Old Testament was compared with the edition chapter by chapter, and every chapter that differed was aligned with Brenton's English verse by verse. The lengths of every verse were also compared with the Church of Greece's verse of the same number, which finds chapters whose numbers match but whose verses begin at other words. A second review read every decision again on all four witnesses, and searched every chapter for divisions the rule calls for, by those lengths and by locating each of Rahlfs's verses in Brenton's English.

## The rule

The edition keeps eBible's numbers, which are Rahlfs's in most chapters, and prints the Church of Greece's wherever a second witness confirms them. It divides a verse otherwise where the Greek editions agree and the edition's division is not Brenton's own, is borrowed, or would put a verse under the wrong number, wherever his English can take the new division.

1. **eBible's number is the base.** A verse keeps eBible's number, which is Rahlfs's in 94% of chapters, unless a later rule changes it. eBible's own letters and slips stand only until rule 2 replaces them. Where eBible keeps the traditional number and the Church of Greece follows Rahlfs, neither rule 2 nor rule 3 applies, so the traditional number stays: Deuteronomy 29 begins at "These are the words of the covenant", as Brenton's printing and Swete begin it, though the Church of Greece and Rahlfs number that verse 28:69.
   - **In the Apocrypha**, that is 1 Esdras, Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch, the Epistle of Jeremias, 1–2 Maccabees, and Daniel's Susanna, Bel, and the Song of the Three Children, eBible follows the King James Apocrypha's division, which no Greek edition uses. There the Church of Greece's number or division replaces eBible's wherever Rahlfs or Swete agrees with it. Brenton's printing has no vote anywhere in these books, even where it departs from the King James division (Tobit 6–7 and 10, Sirach 41), because it is not his own translation there.
2. **The Church of Greece's number replaces the edition's** where Swete agrees with it, or where Brenton's printed page agrees with it with a number of his own. This restores the traditional chapter breaks, and the numbers that eBible lettered or dropped, including a number Swete leaves vacant (Genesis 31:51, Psalm 115:5).
3. **A verse is divided again** where the Church of Greece and Swete agree on the new division, and Rahlfs or Brenton's printed page agrees with them too. A moved boundary, joined verses, and a split verse all count as re-dividing.
   - **Where Brenton's division is his own,** it yields only to keep the numbers in step: where it joins two verses the Greek editions number apart, splits one, letters one, or puts a whole verse under another number, so that a verse looked up by its number would be another verse of the Greek. There the Church of Greece, Swete, and Rahlfs are followed even against his page (Genesis 8:3–5, Leviticus 8:18–30, Abdias 1:1–3). Where they place a verse's edge only part of a verse away from his, by a phrase, a clause, or a sentence, his division stands (Exodus 8:19/20, 3 Kingdoms 12:3/4, Proverbs 15:5/6), and where only the Church of Greece and Swete are against it, it stands too (Genesis 31:53/54).
   - **Where Brenton's division is borrowed,** it has no vote, and the Church of Greece and Swete are enough (3 Kingdoms 11:22/25, Jeremias 36:12/13). In the Apocrypha, the Church of Greece with Rahlfs or with Swete is enough (rule 1).
   - **A division is printed only where Brenton's English can take it.** A verse may begin where his own page already begins one. Otherwise it may begin only where his English begins a sentence or a clause of its own: after his full stop, colon, semicolon, question mark, or exclamation mark, or at a conjunction that opens a clause with its own subject and verb, the subject sometimes implied, as in an imperative. A new verse never begins with a phrase, a list item, or a second verb that belongs to the clause before. His punctuation decides, and where it is ambiguous, the punctuation of his Greek column. A direct quotation counts as beginning a sentence. So Tobit 10/11, Job 23:11/12, 1 Esdras 3:8/9, Jeremias 32:5/6, Susanna 19/20, and 2 Maccabees 1:9/10 keep the edition's division.
4. **A number only the Church of Greece gives**, against Rahlfs, Brenton, and Swete, is presumed a slip and changes nothing. Where its numbers skip one, as at Sirach 29:17–19, the numeral was dropped, not the verse, and the missing number goes where Rahlfs or Swete puts it.
5. **A verse the Church of Greece lacks** keeps the edition's number, unless Brenton and Swete both number it otherwise.
6. **A verse supplied from Codex Alexandrinus** keeps the label its decision gives it: Swete's number from his apparatus or a gap in his numbering, or the number in Brenton's Appendix, wherever those exist. Where the Church of Greece numbers Alexandrinus words that the edition prints inside another verse, they stay inside it, because the passage is supplied whole under one note. So Proverbs 8 runs from 32 to 34, and Proverbs 11 from 3 to 5.

These follow from the rules:

- **Agreement is judged at each verse break**, not over whole verses. Each break is printed or not by the rules above, even where the witnesses differ at the next one.
- **A verse moved across a chapter break** is like any other division: it takes the chapter the witnesses give it, and the chapter's opening words are checked (Tobit 6–7, Genesis 31–32).
- **A chapter never prints the same number twice.** Where the witnesses' number for a verse is already used in the chapter, the verse keeps the edition's label (1 Kingdoms 21:5–16, Jeremias 10:9a).
- **A number may go unused.** Where the verses after a place take the witnesses' numbers but the place itself keeps the edition's division, the edition prints the gap rather than a number no witness gives (Exodus 25 runs from 34 to 36; Jeremias 7 from 26 to 28, where Swete leaves 27 vacant; the Epistle of Jeremias from 15 to 17).
- **A traditional break is restored only where the Church of Greece has it.** Where it follows Rahlfs, a break Brenton and Swete share stays where eBible has it: as Rahlfs has it where eBible follows him (Joel 2/3, Hosea 1/2, Leviticus 5/6), and in its traditional place where eBible keeps that (Deuteronomy 28/29). That is the cost of taking the Church of Greece as the Orthodox standard.
- **A paragraph keeps Brenton's place.** Where a verse now begins elsewhere than his paragraph does, the paragraph still begins where his sense puts it, even inside a verse, since the paragraphs are his and the numbers the Greek editions'.
- **A line of verse keeps its place too.** The lines are placed in Brenton's words as before, and only their indent follows the numbers: a line that opens a verse is set out, as everywhere, so where a verse now begins at another line, that line is set out and the line that opened his verse is indented.

Each change is a decision in `edition/versification.json`. Its reason names its witnesses, with printing and page. Brenton's pages are cited by the physical page of the PDF scan, with the printed page beside it. The 1900 printing's Apocrypha has its own page numbers.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Brenton's printed numbers throughout.** His pages borrow the King James and Hebrew numbers in the Apocrypha, 3 Kingdoms 4–11, Jeremias, the Psalms, and Proverbs. In places they print no number (Jesus the Son of Navi 12:16–22) or the same number twice (13:8). Those cannot be printed as the Septuagint's. The transcriptions are no substitute, since neither keeps his numbers.
- **Swete throughout.** Swete numbers Codex B, so wherever B's text differs from Brenton's, his numbers would divide words Brenton's English lacks or join verses it keeps apart. Modern readers, concordances, and the Greek Orthodox Bible also do not number by him.
- **Rahlfs alone.** It is the modern standard and the smallest change. But at the traditional chapter breaks it follows the Hebrew against Brenton, Swete, and the Church of Greece, and the edition should not be Masoretic.
- **The Church of Greece alone.** It is the Orthodox text, but too many of its website's lone readings are slips. A misprint is very unlikely to agree with Brenton or Swete by chance, so any second witness is enough to confirm a number. Requiring one particular witness would only throw out places that are confirmed.
- **A veto for Brenton's division.** Requiring his page to agree before a verse is divided again would keep his division even where a whole verse of the Greek stands under another number, and would give a vote to divisions that are the King James Bible's or the Hebrew's. The numbering is the Septuagint's, not the translator's. Where his division is his own, a re-division still needs three witnesses, though a relabelling needs two, because a re-division moves notes, links, and lines.
- **The Greek editions at every verse's edge.** Following the three Greek editions wherever they place a verse's edge part of a verse away from Brenton's own would re-cut his sentences in a great many places, and move his notes, the quotation links, and the lines of verse with them, while every verse would still be found under its number. So his own division yields only where a verse would otherwise stand under another number.
- **The Song of the Three Children numbered on its own, 1–67**, as the Church of Greece and Brenton number it. Rahlfs and Swete number it within the chapter, every Septuagint edition cites it so, and separate numbers would need printed labels that differ from the ones the edition uses inside.

## Special places

- **Daniel 3–4** follow Swete. The Song of the Three Children runs on as 3:24–90, in Swete's order, which like Codex B's is out of sequence in three places. Then come 3:91–100 and 4:1–34. Chapter 4 begins where the Church of Greece, Brenton, and Swete all begin it. The Song, Susanna, and Bel are Apocrypha, so rule 1 applies to their divisions.
- **Esther.** Addition A is lettered 1:1a–r, and 1:1 is "And it came to pass after these things", as in Brenton's 1879 printing and Swete. Brenton numbers no verse of Addition A, so the edition keeps its letters.
- **Esaias 9** begins at "Drink this first", as the Church of Greece, Brenton, and Swete begin it. Only the Church of Greece joins Swete's 14 and 15, so from there the verses take Brenton's and Swete's numbers, 9:15–21.
- **Tobit 10–11.** Chapter 11 begins where eBible and Swete begin it, "After these things". The Church of Greece and Rahlfs begin it at "and went on his way", but Brenton's English runs those words on, in one clause, from the words they set at the end of chapter 10 ("and blessed Raguel and Edna his wife, and went on his way").
- **Proverbs 24–31.** The passages the Greek sets in chapter 24 print the Hebrew's numbers, as Rahlfs numbers them, Swete marks them in his margin, the Church of Greece's text marks each passage's first verse in parentheses and numbers the rest by the Hebrew, and Brenton's printing numbers them, though his chapter 30 begins one sentence later, at "These things says the man". The first verse of each passage prints its chapter, as 30:1, 30:15, and 31:1, and the first verse back in chapter 24 prints 24:23. A chapter cannot be numbered twice in the edition, so these verses keep eBible's labels, 24:22f–t, 24:35–53, and 24:54–62, and their notes, links, and decisions use those labels. 24:22a–e keep Rahlfs's letters. In chapter 31, the Greek editions divide Brenton's 24 at "she opens her mouth heedfully". The Church of Greece's text numbers that verse 26 and the next, "She puts on strength", 25, the Hebrew's numbers, which Swete gives in his margin; only Rahlfs numbers them 25 and 26 in Brenton's order. They print 26 before 25, as Brenton's page there prints 27 before 26. A note at 29:27 says that the Hebrew has the three passages there, and that they are printed in chapter 24.
- **Sirach's Prologue** is the book's introduction, with no chapter or verse number. The Church of Greece prints the Prologue unnumbered, and Swete and Rahlfs number only its lines. The book's first verse, "All wisdom", is 1:1.
- **The Epistle of Jeremias's title** is verse 1:0, as Rahlfs numbers it, and prints no number, as in the Church of Greece's text and Swete. The Epistle's verses are 1–72, but for 16: the Greek editions begin it at "their eyes be full of dust", inside Brenton's clause, so 15 runs on to the end of his sentence and 16 is unused.
- **Malachias 4** is printed as the Church of Greece and Swete print it ([Editorial notes](edition.md#divisions-of-the-text)).

## Places the rule leaves as they are

A witness numbers these places otherwise, but the rule keeps the edition's numbers, for the reason given.

- **Proverbs 20:9a–c.** The Church of Greece numbers these sayings 20–22, as Brenton prints them, but Brenton's numbers there are the Hebrew's, Swete numbers them 10–12, and Rahlfs letters them. Brenton's agreement is not with numbers of his own (rule 2), and no other witness agrees with the Church of Greece, so they keep Rahlfs's letters.
- **Exodus 28:23–28.** Brenton's Appendix note on the verses that neither the Vatican nor the Alexandrine copy has quotes them as 23–28, the Hebrew's numbers, while the edition's 28:23–28 are other verses. The note says that its numbers are the Hebrew's.
- **2 Kingdoms 23:37c.** Gelore the Bethorite keeps eBible's letter. Brenton's printing numbers him 37, but the edition's 37 is Elie the Ammanite. The Church of Greece, Swete, and Rahlfs number the two together as 37, but in Brenton's list Adroi and Gadabiel (his 30 and 31) stand between them, so no one verse can hold both.
- **1 Esdras 3:17.** "O ye men, how exceeding strong is wine!" stays in the verse that begins "Then began the first", as the Church of Greece divides it. Rahlfs and Swete begin a verse at "O ye men", but 17 and 18 are confirmed for the verses either side, so no number is free for it.
- **Proverbs 8:33.** Codex Alexandrinus's "And blessed are they that keep my ways. Hear wisdom and be wise, and be not strangers to it" stays inside 8:32, under one note saying the Vatican copy omits it (rule 6). The Church of Greece numbers its second half, "Hear wisdom", 33, as Brenton's Appendix and Swete's apparatus do; dividing it off would split the passage and its note.
- **Sirach 17:9–10.** The Church of Greece and Swete divide eBible's 17:10 in two, but the Church of Greece numbers the clauses 9 and 10 and Swete 10 and 9, so no two witnesses agree on the labels.
- **Sirach 23:7/8 and 41:19–25 (in the Greek editions' numbers), Wisdom 4:14/15 and 7:1/2, Esther 9:10/11, Proverbs 7:6/7, Lamentations 3:52/53, Ezekiel 21:21/22, Jeremias 23:6/9, and Esaias 60:5/6.** The Greek editions agree (in Sirach, the Church of Greece and Rahlfs) on a division that would begin a verse inside Brenton's clause or with an item of his list, so his English cannot take it (rule 3), as at the places rule 3 names.
- **Sirach 30–36.** The Church of Greece and Rahlfs number these chapters by the Latin order of the exchanged blocks. Swete prints the blocks in that order too, but keeps the manuscripts' chapter and verse numbers, which Brenton and the edition follow; numbering by the Latin order would print chapters 30, 33, and 36 twice. Inside these chapters the verses are numbered and divided by rule 1, like the rest of Sirach, wherever the witnesses' numbers can stand in the edition's chapter; a number of the other arrangement cannot (33:11–12).
- **Jeremias 29–30.** The Church of Greece sets Edom in chapter 29 and numbers Ammon, Kedar, and Damascus 30:1–16, as Swete does. Its Edom numbers rest only on Brenton's Hebrew numbers, which print 29:7 twice, and it runs Edom's first verse into its own 29:7, so the edition keeps its numbers.
- **Jeremias 10:9a.** The Church of Greece, Brenton's page, and Rahlfs number this verse 5 a second time, and Swete letters it 5b. A chapter never prints a label twice, so it keeps eBible's letter.

## How the build carries it out

- **The Assemble stage applies the numbering**, after the readings from Codex Alexandrinus and the CCEL notes have been placed by eBible's numbering. So a note's `x-key`, the decisions about Codex Alexandrinus, and the corrections and notes of Brenton's sources name eBible's verse. Every decision read after Assemble names the edition's verse.
- **`relabel`** maps a run of eBible's labels onto the edition's. Where a relabelled verse begins a chapter, and did not begin one before, the decision gives the words the chapter opens with.
- **`breaks`** divides a run of verses again where the decision's words begin, each part labelled with its printed verse. The words must occur once in the run, and are spelled as in eBible, since revision comes later. The first part begins where the run does.
- **`unnumbered`** names a verse that prints no number; a chapter none of whose verses is numbered prints no number either. A relocated passage (`relocations`) prints the Hebrew's labels. Both set the label the verse prints (USFM `\vp`), which only PTXprint, the margin notes, and the website read. Everything else names the verse by its own label.
- **The Updated Brenton** is numbered as eBible is, and its labels are mapped the same way before its lines are placed. Its words are not divided again, so a division that sets words in another chapter is refused in its poems.
- **Brenton's citations** in his notes are read by eBible's numbering where the edition keeps it. A citation of a verse that the edition relabels or divides again could mean his printed number or eBible's, so a decision in `edition/citations.json` names the verse meant.
- **The build refuses** a decision whose verses or words it cannot find, or that changes nothing; a verse relabelled twice, or relabelled and divided; a chapter whose opening words don't match; a citation by Brenton's numbers of a verse the edition relabels or divides again, which no decision names; and a citation, quotation link, comparison, or passage on the edition's own pages of a verse that prints another label than its own.
