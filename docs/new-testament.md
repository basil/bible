# The New Testament text

The [editorial notes](edition.md) describe the edition as a whole. This page gives the account of its New Testament: how the King James Version is conformed to the Byzantine text of Robinson and Pierpont (2026), what counts as a difference that English can show, the rules by which the English is changed, and the cases that test them. [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) says how the build carries this out, and [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md#reviewing-the-byzantine-readings) how the readings are reviewed.

## Why this is a reconciliation problem

The King James translators followed the Received Text, the Greek of the printed editions of Erasmus, Stephanus and Beza, as F. H. A. Scrivener reconstructed it from their choices in 1894. The Orthodox Church reads the Byzantine text, which the great majority of the Greek manuscripts carry, and which Maurice A. Robinson and William G. Pierpont edited in 2005, 2018 and 2026. Scrivener's text and RP2026 differ in 1,939 places. Most cannot be seen in English. Which ones can, and what the King James words should be instead, has already been decided in print, several times over, by people working independently:

| Question | Who has answered it | Form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where does Scrivener differ from RP? | Robinson's collation (1,885 places); Boyd's TCGNT apparatus (1,948 notes); a token comparison of the two texts | Greek, unit by unit |
| Which differences show in English, and how, in the King James words? | William G. Pierpont, _Some Improvements to the King James Version from the Majority Greek Manuscripts_ (1990, 960 instructions with manuscript weights) | King James English: find these words, put these |
| Which differences show in English at all? | Boyd's TCENT apparatus (552 notes); Thomason's _Far Above All_ with every TR variant inline (862); the MSB and WEB footnotes | Modern English, each to a stated standard |
| Did a revision of the KJV make the same change? | The Revised Version of 1881; Boyd's ASV conformed to RP2018 (2021) | Full revisions, text and style together |

So the build does not translate. It reads these sources mechanically, lines them up unit by unit against the pinned Greek and the pinned King James text, carries out what binds and fits, and leaves the rest to the editor's decisions in `edition/byzantine.json`. Pierpont is the primary English witness: he co-edited the Robinson–Pierpont text and wrote in the King James words. Where an instruction cannot be applied or its wording fails the house style, a fitting KJV parallel is preferred, then Pierpont's wording, the Revised Version of 1881, and Robert Adam Boyd's _American Standard Version, Byzantine Text_ (2021). Boyd's _Text-Critical English New Testament_ (2022) and Thomason's _Far Above All_ are occasionally consulted, in that order. Borrowed wording is adapted into King James English. [sources/README.md](../sources/README.md#the-new-testament-greek-and-its-witnesses) lists every source.

## The trust model

The units are the places where the two Greek texts differ, as the token comparison, the collation and Boyd's apparatus find them. Two decisions in `edition/byzantine.json` add to what they find, each checked against the sources: a difference of accent or breathing alone, which the unaccented texts cannot show, is made a unit where it changes the sense (1 Corinthians 5:13, Mark 4:8 and 20); and Hodges–Farstad's side is given where no apparatus the build reads gives it (Philemon 1:1).

Every Greek unit gets exactly one **disposition**, decided in this order:

1. **override**: a reading in `edition/byzantine.json` covers the unit, either with edits or with a `nochange` ruling.
2. **structural**: a whole-verse difference. Luke 17:36, Acts 8:37, 15:34 and 24:7 are omitted, each quoted in a note on the verse before; Romans 16:25–27 stands at 14:24–26, with a note at each place; Matthew 23:13 and 14 exchange places. These are decisions in `edition/byzantine.json` too, held to both texts' inventories: the omitted verses are exactly those RP2018 numbers without words, and the moves account for every address one text has and the other lacks.
3. **witnessed**: a published King James-worded instruction _binds_ uniquely to the pinned King James text, _attaches_ to the unit (its changed words bridge through the CrossWire alignment to the unit's Scrivener tokens, or the verse has one unit of fitting shape), and is _compatible_ with the unit's Greek (an omission where RP is shorter, an addition where it is longer, a transposition at a reordering, a replacement anywhere but pure orthography). Its words are also checked against RP2026's main text: an omission may not remove words for Greek its unit keeps (Rev 7:5); an addition may not leave the verse with more of a word than RP2026 has Greek to render (Rev 4:8, three "holy", not nine); a change may not touch only Greek that RP2026's main text shares with the King James text's, as where an instruction follows a reading RP2018 prints in its margin (Rev 3:1, 8:3, 13:10); and a unit that only reorders words admits only an English reordering. The evidence is CrossWire's tagging of the whole King James Version, which says which English words render which Greek lemma; it shows lexical support, not that a rendering is right, so each executed instruction is tagged `ev:lexically-supported` or `ev:lexically-unresolved` and the review judges the English.
4. **neutral**: the difference is spelling, movable ν/ς or word division, and no witness reports it.
5. **silent**: everything else. The English stands.

Instructions are judged and carried out by **construction**, never in parts. A source's instructions whose edits in a verse share a unit, or whose words overlap or touch, form one group, checked on its combined effect (a word one instruction removes and another restores is a move) and carried out whole or not at all. A row that does not bind but is attached to a unit is a member too, and blocks its group (Rev 6:11). An override that touches a construction takes it whole, and one override owns a construction; an instruction spanning verses is one construction in all of them (Acts 9:5–6). So the finished sentence is always one source's wording, or the editor's, never a splice of fragments. Two overlapping, differing instructions from the deciding source are a **conflict**, and nothing is carried out.

Nothing else gates execution. Corroboration, Pierpont's weight, Hodges–Farstad's side, RP's marginal alternates, the Patriarchal text, the revision alarm (both revisions lack the King James words for an unreported unit) and an unplaced witness row in the verse are **tags**, and the tags give each unit a controversy score, which the review shows.

The executor applies the chosen edits at fixed offsets in the King James USJ with four seam rules (the capital of a word that begins or stops beginning a sentence; punctuation an omission leaves behind; _a_/_an_ before a changed word; an instruction's bracketed supplied words become supplied words) and refuses by name anything else. An independent check then recomputes every verse from the declared edits and compares it with the pinned King James text character by character (the invariants listed in `src/bible/byzantine/verify.py`). Every edited verse is then read as a whole: a join an edit leaves, or a note that, put back, does not give the King James words exactly, is tagged `ev:finished-verse` and listed in the review.

## The notes

Every change carries a note, marked with the edition's abbreviation for the Received Text, in the [style of the edition's notes](edition.md#notes): the words it is about, a colon, and what the Received Text has. `fruit: TR _fruits_` replaces the lemma with the Received Text's words; `said: TR adds _by them of old time_` puts them after it; `verily: TR omits` takes it out, and `but who: TR omits _but_` takes out a part of it, as Brenton's "Vat. omits" quotes only the part of its lemma that is omitted. The short forms are used only where they give back the King James words exactly; otherwise the note quotes the King James words for a lemma that spans the change (`lepers, cast: TR _lepers, raise the dead, cast_`). The lemma is the smallest phrase that names the change and occurs once in the verse, widened to keep a determiner with its noun, and widened by hand in `edition/byzantine.json` where the rule chooses badly.

An omitted verse is quoted whole in a note on the verse before it (`and the other left: TR adds verse 36 _Two men shall be in the field…_`), and a moved passage is noted at both its places (`TR has this passage at 16:25–27` at Romans 14:24; `TR has here the verses printed at 14:24–26` at Romans 16:24). The Old Testament's passages that the Greek sets in another order than the Hebrew are noted the same way, with the Hebrew's label ([Divisions of the text](edition.md#divisions-of-the-text)).

A 1611 marginal note whose reading is the Byzantine reading the text now prints (Matthew 26:26 "Many Greek copies have, gave thanks"; Revelation 13:1 "Or, names"), or which glosses words of the Received Text that the edition no longer has (Matthew 9:36 "Or, were tired and lay down" for "fainted"), is left out, by a decision in `edition/kjv-notes.json` with its reason; the Received Text's words stand in the TR note. The 1611 translators' own remark on Luke 17:36, that the verse is wanting in most Greek copies, stands at 17:35 beside the note that quotes the verse.

## What counts as a translatable difference

Several scholars have decided which Greek differences can be seen in English, each by a stated standard. The edition keeps the King James Version's literalness: a difference is translatable when ordinary King James English shows it, in meaning, reference, number, tense, mood, voice, emphasis or grammatical relation, even if the sense of the passage is unchanged. An article, particle, inflection or word order does not by itself need its own English; a revised passage is no more literal than its neighbours.

**Pierpont** notes "only those places where the change is translatable, or potentially translatable", in the King James words, and excludes words the translators supplied with no basis in any text, places where the King James Version already agrees with the Majority against the Received Text (he lists thirty), and mere improvements of rendering. He gives each change a weight by manuscript support; the weight is a tag, not a standard of translatability. **Boyd**'s TCENT notes only what shows in an "optimal equivalence" translation and ignores "minor differences involving word order, phrasing, conjunctions, articles, prepositions, and the like", so his list is a lower bound: a difference he notes shows in English, though the King James wording may already carry it. **Thomason**'s _Far Above All_ is hyper-literal and marks a contrast wherever his English can carry one, so his list is close to an upper bound. The **MSB** and **WEB** footnotes are selections: a note is evidence, silence is not. From the loosest to the strictest about what must be reported: Boyd, then the WEB and MSB, then Pierpont, then Thomason.

| Who reports | Meaning | Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pierpont, bound and compatible | Proposed King James wording, subject to the house style | witnessed |
| Boyd’s TCENT, and no King James-worded instruction | Shows in English; the King James wording may already carry it | silent, `ev:tcent-reports`: a priority for review, normally a reading |
| Only Thomason, the MSB or the WEB | Possibly below the King James Version's resolution | silent, `ev:hyper-literal-only` |
| Nobody | No witness reports an English contrast | silent or neutral |

What nobody counts as translatable, when the witnesses are also silent: movable ν and ς; word division and crasis; accents, breathings and punctuation except where they change the word (Philippians 3:5, 1 Corinthians 5:13, Luke 3:23); spellings of common names (Δαυίδ/Δαβίδ, Μωσῆς/Μωϋσῆς); variant forms of one inflection (εἶπον/εἶπαν). Two that look like spelling and are not: a changed verbal prefix (Acts 3:24), and a transliterated word inside a quotation (Matthew 27:46 λαμά/λιμά).

The Revised Version of 1881 and Boyd's ASV of 2021 are full revisions, never compared with the King James Version to find readings: of some 6,400 verses with no Greek difference, only about 720 are word-identical between the King James Version and Boyd's ASV. A revision's change is read only where the CrossWire bridge places it on the words of one unit, or where it makes the same contrast as a placed instruction; it corroborates an edit, raises the alarm when both revisions lack the King James words of an unreported unit, and supplies wording in King James diction when a reading needs it. Boyd's method for his revision is the model: "every effort has been made to stay within the style and vocabulary of the ASV. When available, alternate renderings in the ASV footnotes were used. Otherwise, modifications to the text were most often derived from the King James Version itself."

Facts about the Received Text that affect the reading of any list: in 152 units Scrivener has a reading against RP that Stephanus does not share, so everything is checked against Scrivener; in 365 units the Received Text's reading is RP's own marginal alternate, and in 528 the Patriarchal text of 1904 reads with the Received Text. The target is RP2026's main text; these bear on how contested a change is, and are tags.

## The English house style

**Least disturbance, not fewest words.** A changed case, referent or clause relation may take its whole phrase with it; dependent words, agreement and punctuation move only where the change breaks them. A hard reading stays hard, and an ambiguous one ambiguous: do not explain it away, harmonize it with a parallel or mend it, but put its construction in grammatical King James English.

**Wording.** Start from the selected instruction, where one exists. If none executes or its wording fails the house style, decide with, in order, a King James parallel that fits the sense and grammar here, the words of Pierpont, the Revised Version or Boyd's ASV in King James diction, and only then the editor's own; the reading's `from:` tag says which.

**Diction.** The King James vocabulary, spelling, pronouns and inflections: _thou_, _thee_, _thy_, _thine_, nominative _ye_, _-eth_ and _-est_. Choose a word for its meaning, not its age; it need not occur elsewhere in the King James Version. Do not modernize, and do not force one English word on a Greek lemma.

**Verbs.** A changed tense, mood or voice is rendered as the King James Version renders it in context, not by formula. Judge like constructions together: conditionals, negation, dependent clauses.

**Reference.** A changed name, pronoun or possessive is rendered where it shows at the King James Version's level. Do not put a name for a pronoun to clarify it, or explain in a parenthesis.

**Order.** Carry changed coordination, attachment and emphasis where English syntax allows. Move existing phrases rather than rewrite them; do not copy Greek order into unnatural English.

**Capitals and supplied words.** The King James capitals stay unless a changed expression or sentence boundary needs another; no new reverential capitals. A King James supplied word for which RP2026 now has Greek is no longer marked supplied; necessary supplied English is marked, judged by the Greek construction and King James usage; supplied words that served only an omitted construction go with it. (The edition prints supplied words in ordinary type, so the marking shows only in the sources sent to be typeset.)

## The cases

The verses that test the executor's seam rules and the trust model, with what the edition does at each. The reading probes in `tests/test_byzantine_cases.py` hold the edition to them.

**Greek differs, English stands.** Matthew 1:1 Δαβίδ/Δαυίδ, a proper-name spelling; John 18:32 ἤμελλεν/ἔμελλεν, a verb spelling; Matthew 5:23 κἀκεῖ/καὶ ἐκεῖ, word division; Acts 19:29 and Mark 11:22, an article with a proper name; Mark 7:24 "a house" and Revelation 9:20, an article with a common noun; Matthew 18:4, a subjunctive for a future the King James "shall humble" already carries; Matthew 12:21, a preposition omitted in Greek that English still requires.

**Omissions.** Matthew 3:11 "and _with_ fire" goes with its supplied word; 5:27 "by them of old time"; 10:8 "raise the dead," with its comma; 27:35, where the colon before the omitted fulfilment quotation becomes a full stop; Luke 3:19 "his brother Philip's wife" becomes "his brother's wife"; 9:1 "his twelve disciples" becomes "the twelve"; John 18:11 "thy sword" becomes "the sword"; Acts 9:5–6, one construction across two verses; 12:23 "God the glory" becomes "glory to God" after the article goes; 20:21 "Christ" goes with no space before the stop; 2 Corinthians 11:31 "of our Lord" becomes "of the Lord"; 1 John 5:7–8, where two verses become one sentence and the punctuation is part of the decision; Revelation 1:8, two units, a clause removed and "God" added; 6:3 "and see", here only.

**Additions and substitutions.** Matthew 4:10 "Get thee hence, Satan" becomes "Get thee behind me, Satan", as the King James Version renders the same Greek at Luke 4:8; 18:19 "Again, verily I say"; 26:26 "blessed _it_" becomes "gave thanks"; 27:41 "and Pharisees"; Luke 6:9 "to destroy _it_" becomes "to kill"; 8:31 "they besought" becomes "he besought"; Acts 3:24 "foretold of" becomes "announced"; Acts 8:16 and 2 Timothy 2:19, where the article changes with the title; 1 Peter 3:1 "may … be won" becomes "shall … be won"; Ephesians 4:6, 1 Thessalonians 4:8 and 2 Corinthians 1:11, where first and second person plural swap; Revelation 20:2, a relative clause that closes before the main clause resumes; 22:21 "our Lord" becomes "the Lord" and "you all" becomes "all the saints".

**Several units in one verse.** 1 Peter 5:10 "us" becomes "you" and three of four verbs change from optative to future; Revelation 13:5 "blasphemies" becomes "blasphemy" and "war" is added; Luke 22:30 omits "in my kingdom" and makes "sit" future; Revelation 9:10, one recast predicate covering several units; 11:10 "shall rejoice" becomes "rejoice", "make merry" gains its own "shall", "send" becomes "give"; 5:14 omits "four and twenty" and "him that liveth for ever and ever".

**Reorderings, transliterations, quotations.** Luke 8:51, Matthew 21:14 and Revelation 1:18, one reordered phrase each, applied as one transposition; Revelation 22:8, a phrase replacement that carries the reordering and removes the supplied object; Philippians 2:21, a unit (an article) at which the names stand in the same order in both texts, so a directive must attach to its own unit; Matthew 27:46 λαμά/λιμά and Mark 15:34, a spelling difference inside a transliterated quotation that does change the English; Matthew 21:1 and Mark 11:1 "Bethphage" becomes "Bethsphage"; John 1:28 "Bethabara" becomes "Bethany"; Romans 16:3 "Priscilla" becomes "Prisca".

**Construction judgments.** Acts 18:17 keeps "cared" by a `nochange` ruling; Matthew 12:6 keeps "one greater" for the neuter comparative; Revelation 21:16's added second "twelve" takes supplied "each" and "thousand furlongs" to carry the elliptical measurement, and its note restores the King James comparison whole.

**Machine output to recognise.** If the executor ever emits anything of this shape, a seam rule is missing: "and given thanks" (an auxiliary left out), "a borders" (an article moved without its noun), "shall suffer not suffer", "All. the saints" (punctuation kept inside a replaced phrase), "Thy sins be forgiven Your" (a donor's pronoun in a King James sentence), a lower-case sentence start after a removed "And".
