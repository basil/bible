# Verification

Checks come in two kinds. Guards inside `scripts/pipeline.py` run on every build and refuse to produce a PDF from damaged or unexpected input. Tests under `tests/` hold the edition's editorial policy and exercise the guards themselves. `make test` runs the tests and `make check` runs them before its builds; CI runs them on pushes to `master` and on pull requests.

`make test-python` runs [pytest](https://docs.pytest.org/) over `tests/`, configured in `pyproject.toml`. The tests read the pinned sources and prepare every scripture unit, but typeset nothing, so they take seconds. `tests/test_edition.py` holds the editorial policy described below, `tests/test_helpers.py` tests the text helpers on small synthetic USFM, and `tests/test_faults.py` holds the fault-injection checks. `make test-tex` runs the [l3build](https://ctan.org/pkg/l3build) regression test `tests/tex/protrusion.lvt`, configured in `build.lua`, which compares the plain-XeTeX protrusion customization against stock XeLaTeX microtype for every present glyph in the edition's font families, faces, and representative sizes. `tests/tex/protrusion.tlg` records the expected result, no mismatches for each font; any mismatch is logged per glyph and fails the test.

`make validate`, which every build also runs first, verifies archive SHA-256 hashes, each original USFM member's hash, every chapter and verse label, and all marker counts. All original USFM files, including excluded files, are checked against their locked inventories. It checks the SHA-256 hash of the marginal-notes transcription and parses exactly 775 New Testament notes; each configured anchor and correction must be used, and no transcriber's bracketed remark may remain.

`tests/test_edition.py` enforces the selection of all 52 Brenton and 27 KJV scripture sources, the Orthodox order, and the positions of the section dividers, each testament's front matter, and the appendices. The front-matter order requires Brenton's preface, introduction, introduction to the Apocrypha, and abbreviations between the Old Testament divider and Genesis, and the KJV dedication and translators' preface between the New Testament divider and Matthew. It checks each Brenton title against the edition's list of revised titles or Brenton's own `toc1`, and verifies that every `toc1` long title matches the edition manifest while `toc2` and `h` use the configured short names and the `mt1`-`mt3` heading lines, joined in order, spell the long title, with Brenton's books laid out as the manifest's `heading` directs. It permits no periods in `h`, `toc1`, or `mt1`-`mt3`, and checks the Saint Paul, Catholic epistle, and Gospel headings, the generated `BookNames.xml`, and the Jeremias table titles. It allows straight quotes in `config/front.sfm` and `config/introduction.sfm` only inside USFM attribute values.

Preparation enforces the Ezra-Nehemias split, Daniel section grouping, and Malachias 4 labels, and compares transformed passages, notes, and styling against their pinned source passages. Each marginal note must anchor to exactly one place in its verse. Removing the inserted notes must give back the unchanged Cambridge text, and each note's `fr` reference must match the verse that holds it. Preparation checks that peripheral ID remapping leaves chapter/verse inventories and all source marker counts intact, and that the quotation tags and note spacer leave the printable content unchanged. It expands `S.` to `Saint` in titles and drops their closing full stops. It records quotation tags, the reversible pipe escape, and the nonprinting spacer needed for the reference-only note. Before SmartyPants curls the quotes of each source-derived unit, preparation refuses text that SmartyPants would misread (an HTML-like `<`, a backslash escape, doubled quote characters, or a backtick that does not open a word). Afterwards it requires the same markers and verses, the same text once quotation marks, ellipses, and dashes are folded back to their plain forms, and no remaining straight quote; the converted text is the baseline for the later printable-content comparisons. After PTXprint preprocessing, each unit is compared against the generated project for exact printable content (ignoring only markup and whitespace), exact chapter/verse inventories, and counts of notes, references, italics, tables, and printed verse markers. The generated order is recorded explicitly. Source originals and processed project files are retained for comparison.

PDF verification uses qpdf, pdfinfo, pdffonts, pdftohtml, and pdftotext. It checks every page's A5 dimensions, font embedding, expected text, verse-number, and quotation fonts, that the added words in Malachias 4:2 share the font of the roman words around them, that the extracted text has no straight quotation mark or backtick, inherited publication text, and missing-glyph/TeX failures. The contents must converge between TeX runs, list every selected unit exactly once in manifest order, display the correct page numbers in the PDF, and point to pages containing the actual unit headings. Rendered-text witnesses check the dedication's opening and ending, the translators' preface, the Acts closing line, and all 14 traditional epistle subscriptions. Wording witnesses use PDF content-stream order because geometric text extraction can interleave the two columns when characters protrude into the gutter. Witness matching also checks a copy with numbered running headers removed, so page breaks cannot insert page furniture into a phrase; the separate layout extraction is retained for page and contents checks. `make check` builds twice with separate clean project and home/cache directories, checks matching text and page counts, and renders every page at 72 dpi for matching raster SHA-256 hashes. PDF metadata bytes need not match. Repeatability checks do not replace human proofreading.

`tests/test_faults.py` damages one input at a time and requires the matching guard to refuse it: a wrong font or source archive checksum, a changed archive member, member list, or inventory, a changed marginal-notes checksum, a missing, wrong, or ambiguous note anchor, an unused or inapplicable correction, a transcriber's remark left in a note, a manifest that disagrees with the Ezra-Nehemias split or the Daniel grouping, a moved Malachias or Song of the Three Children boundary, and footnotes already present in the Cambridge text. For PTXprint's processed output, it requires the comparison to reject a deleted footnote, a dropped character style, and a relabelled verse.

Visual inspection must cover dense footnotes and their callers, the New Testament marginal notes (callers before the glossed words, and the restored Greek in Acts 13:18 and 13:34), Greek and RTL Hebrew quotations, poetic indentation, the long Jeremias table title and table rows, contents, Ezra-Nehemias and Malachias chapter boundaries, the Daniel sections, and the transitions between the Old Testament, the New Testament, and the appendices, including each testament's front matter. See [the visual inspection record](inspection.md) for page references and findings.
